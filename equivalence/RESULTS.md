## Tolerance, fixed before measuring

Both implementations do the same three steps in IEEE 754 double precision:
subtract a per-gene mean, take a dot product over a few hundred genes, then
`atan2`. Each step carries a relative error of order 1e-16, and summing a few
hundred terms in a different order costs at most a few more units in the last
place. An agreement of order 1e-13 is therefore what a correct port should
give, and anything at 1e-6 or above indicates a real difference in the
computation, not rounding.

Three bounds are scored, all fixed before measuring:

| Bound | Value | Why this value |
|---|---|---|
| median circular difference | 1e-09 rad | Four orders of magnitude above the expected 1e-13, so ordinary rounding passes, and four orders below the 1e-6 scale at which a real difference shows. |
| maximum circular difference | 1e-06 rad | Per-cell worst case, not a summary. A subpopulation that inverted would blow past it. |
| maximum arc error | 1e-09 | Circular difference times projected radius. `atan2` is ill conditioned near the origin, so a cell sitting almost exactly at the embedding centre can show a large angular difference from a floating-point-sized positional difference. This bound measures the positional difference itself and stays meaningful there. |

The gene means are checked first, at 1e-12. If the two sides did not
centre the same numbers, the angle comparison answers a different question.
